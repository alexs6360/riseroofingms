Signs of Storm Damage

Hail — what to look for

  • Dark circular bruises where granules have been knocked off
  • Granules collecting in gutters and downspouts
  • Dents on soft metal: vents, flashing, gutters, AC fins
  • Cracked or split shingles

Hail damage is often invisible from the ground — it takes a close inspection to find.

Wind — what to look for

  • Lifted, curled, or creased shingles
  • Missing shingles or exposed underlayment
  • Debris impact damage
  • Leaks appearing days or weeks after the storm

Wind damage spreads. One lifted shingle lets water under the ones around it.

What to Do After a Storm

  1. Document what you can safely see from the ground. Photograph debris and any water stains inside the house.
  2. Call us to tarp the roof. Don't climb up yourself — a storm-damaged roof is unstable. We'll secure it, document the damage, and keep the receipts your insurer may ask for.
  3. Get a professional inspection before you call your insurer, so you know what you are actually reporting.
  4. File the claim. Policies set their own deadline for filing after the date of loss.
  5. Have your contractor on site when the adjuster inspects.

Deadlines and coverage vary from policy to policy. Check your own policy or ask your agent — nothing on this page is a statement about what your insurer will or will not pay.
